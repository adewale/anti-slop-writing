Verdict: ask-author
Slop tells: "doubled activation in ten days," "identified each user's intent," and "made the product feel immediately useful" ask the reader to trust a metric, a mechanism, and a sentiment claim without evidence.
Specificity missing: Define activation; name the baseline, cohort, measurement source, and ten-day window; explain how the personalization engine identified intent and how the checklist changed.
Inflated claim: The paragraph turns an unsupported outcome into a clean cause-and-effect story.
Flow break: It states the result before showing the measurement or the mechanism that produced it.
Concrete rewrite: Ask author: What activation event doubled, from what baseline, over which users, and where is the measurement? Which engine or intent signal changed the checklist? If those answers are not available, cut to: The onboarding redesign changed the checklist. Add activation data and intent evidence before claiming it doubled activation or made the product immediately useful.
Rewrite check: The fallback contains no invented tool, model, source, experiment, count, or timing. It avoids rule-of-three, X-not-Y contrast, em-dash antithesis, banned phrases, prestige adjectives, and decorative closure; passes self-detectors.
Remembered line: Do not let the metric stand in for its measurement.
