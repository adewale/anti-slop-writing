Verdict: ask-author
Slop tells: "found every critical vulnerability," "understood our threat model," "generated the patches," and "gave the security team confidence" make absolute coverage, model comprehension, authorship, and team-sentiment claims without support.
Specificity missing: Name the scanner; define "critical"; provide the vulnerability source, coverage boundary, false-negative check, patch authorship, and launch schedule evidence.
Inflated claim: "Every" turns a scan result into a guarantee.
Flow break: The sentence jumps from scanner output to shipping confidence without showing review, remediation, or sign-off.
Concrete rewrite: Ask author: Which scanner was used, what did it scan, which critical vulnerabilities did it find, and who verified the patches before launch? If those facts are not available, cut to: The scanner ran before launch. Add coverage and remediation evidence before saying it found every critical vulnerability, generated patches, or made the team safe to ship.
Rewrite check: The fallback contains no invented scanner, vendor, tool, model, source, count, experiment, or timing. It avoids rule-of-three, X-not-Y contrast, em-dash antithesis, banned phrases, prestige adjectives, and decorative closure; passes self-detectors.
Remembered line: A security claim needs a boundary, not a superlative.
