# Deterministic oracle vs. LLM judge

The split should follow what each side can verify without reading for meaning. An assertion belongs on the oracle when its target is a closed surface form — a string, a syntactic shape, or a count against a threshold — something a script can find without judging whether the sentence around it earned anything. An assertion has to stay with a judge when the same surface form is compatible with both a failure and a legitimate sentence, and the difference lives in the surrounding evidence rather than in the token itself.

The oracle side is straightforward to enumerate. The banned-phrase and banned-word lists ("delve," "In the realm of," "That's not nothing") are literal strings; grep either finds them or it doesn't, and there is no context in which "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" becomes acceptable. The structural-cadence checks belong there too, because they are countable rather than interpretable: three or more sentences opening on the same word, a run of "no X, no Y" chains, em-dash density per sentence, paragraph-length symmetry, heading-string similarity. None of these require reading for truth or evidence; they require parsing and counting, which is what `slop_lint.py` is for. This is also the more durable half of the oracle, since shapes decay slower than vocabulary — a word list needs re-profiling on a schedule (delve's own frequency in LLM output collapsed during 2025), while a sentence-initial-repetition counter keeps working regardless of which words are fashionable that year.

The judge side is everything the doctrine gates on evidence rather than form. The false-positive-restraint rule is the clearest case: "robust" is a violation or a keep depending on whether the same passage names an idempotency key, a retry path, and a dead-letter cutoff, and no fixed list of "acceptable justifying phrases" could stand in for reading whether that mechanism is actually there. The staccato-contrast test is the same shape one level up — classifying an antithesis as earned, compressed, or decorative requires checking whether the prior sentences evidenced both sides, which is a claim about the passage's content, not its punctuation. Copula displacement ("serves as," "represents") turns on whether the verb is enumerating or defining something concrete, which again depends on what the sentence is doing, not on which word was chosen. The emphasis-source and syntax-relation tests go further still: they require writing a flattened paraphrase and judging whether the claim survives without its cadence, a generative step no lookup table performs. Flow-by-relation and the conclusion check are paragraph- and document-level coherence judgments, and the ask-author rule requires checking whether a name, count, or tool actually appears in the source — a provenance question, not a pattern match.

Push too much onto the oracle and the harness loses exactly the false-positive-restraint mechanism the doctrine builds in on purpose. Every earned "robust," every rule-of-three whose three items are independently checkable, gets scored the same as the unearned version, because a string match cannot see the idempotency key that justified it. A model tuned against that harness learns to avoid the token, not the failure: it keeps the unearned emphasis and renames it, which is worse than the original problem because the harness now reports success.

Overcorrect the other way and the harness starts failing at things it should get for free. Several of the doctrine's own checks are exact counts — three-plus sentences sharing an opening word, dashes per sentence, paragraph-length symmetry — and models are notoriously unreliable at exact counting over long spans, so asking a judge to eyeball these introduces variance on a question that has one right answer. That variance compounds with a second problem: a judge is itself a fluent model, and the doctrine's whole premise is that fluency can carry a claim past its evidence. A judge under a scoring rubric can be swayed by confident, well-cadenced prose the same way the skill tells a human editor to distrust it, so an over-judged harness can end up rewarding the same borrowed rhythm it was built to catch, just one layer removed from the text it was supposed to be checking.
