# Reading a detector-list drift pattern

Zero hits on `ai-vocabulary` across fifty runs looks like the detector doing its job, but the doctrine already has a name for this exact shape: it is what happened to `delve`, whose frequency in LLM output "dropped sharply" during 2025 and which the skill keeps on record as the cautionary case of a word list that outlived its target. A word list can only catch the specific tokens it was built against. Once model output moves off "crucial," "transformative," "underscore," and the rest of that generation's tells, the detector doesn't report clean; it reports obsolete, and those two outcomes produce an identical hit count. Before treating the zero as good news, the less interesting explanation needs ruling out first — that the check itself broke (a stale regex, a disabled rule) rather than that the writing genuinely moved. An oracle can only return a number; it cannot say which of those two stories produced it, which is exactly why fifty consecutive zeros calls for a human or judge-level look rather than an automatic pass.

The rising `significance-compression` count is the more informative half of the same pattern, because the doctrine already predicts it. The 2026-08 additions — "that's the whole point," "is the entire business model" — are named as new-register replacements for the same failure the old vocabulary list used to catch: unearned importance asserted instead of earned. What moved is the costume, not the habit. A model that used to reach for "pivotal" now reaches for "that's not nothing," and the two phrases are doing the same job in the sentence. Read this way, the fifty-run trend is not two unrelated detector lines, one dying and one growing; it is one failure mode migrating across the lexical layer while the harness's coverage of it widens to follow.

For the detector lists, the right move is retirement rather than deletion. Demote `ai-vocabulary` to a low-weight legacy check the way the doctrine already treats `delve` — documented as the known case, kept around cheaply in case a model family reverts to it, no longer load-bearing for pass/fail. Deleting it outright would lose the ability to notice a rebound, and rebounds happen when a later model generation trains on a corpus that reintroduces an old habit nobody is watching for anymore. At the same time, `significance-compression` should not be treated as the permanent replacement: the doctrine says outright that the 2026-08 register "will drift the same way." The useful trigger for the next re-profiling pass is the hit-rate curve itself, not a fixed calendar date — a lexical detector approaching zero while a sibling detector in the same doctrine layer keeps climbing is the same signal this fifty-run window just produced, only visible a step ahead of the zero instead of after it.

For the doctrine, this is a confirming case for the layering it already commits to. The core claim — that AI writing substitutes borrowed importance for earned detail — is the stable thing under test, and the word lists are disposable instruments for catching whichever surface form that substitution currently takes. If the harness had wired its pass/fail decision directly to the `ai-vocabulary` list instead of to that underlying judgment, this drift would have made the eval quietly always pass at the exact moment a sibling list shows the failure is still live. The fix isn't a bigger word list; it's keeping the score tied to the judgment the lists exist to approximate, so that when one word list ages out the way `delve` did, the eval notices the gap in coverage instead of quietly reporting the doctrine solved.
