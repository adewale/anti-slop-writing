# Critique: "Rollout gating relies on soft-quorum drains, so tenant-affinity pools never see a partial config. Because the drain completes before the flip, a bad config is caught in staging rather than in production."

## Trial 1

Verdict: ask-author
Slop tells: "Soft-quorum drains" and "tenant-affinity pools" are coined compound labels — hyphenated, plural, confidently unglossed. Neither is standard shorthand the way "write-ahead log" or "copy-on-write" is, and the paragraph never opens either one up. "Caught in staging rather than in production" is also a staccato "X rather than Y" contrast; applying the staccato contrast test, neither side is evidenced by the sentence that precedes it (which only establishes an ordering, not a detection step), so the contrast supplies closure without content.
Specificity missing: Sentence 1 states the effect of the drain (pools never see a partial config) but not its mechanism — what "soft" modifies in "soft-quorum," what makes a pool "tenant-affinity" rather than just a pool. Sentence 2 never names what does the catching. A drain reorders traffic; it doesn't inspect a config value. Nothing here names a schema check, a canary probe, or a test.
Inflated claim: "A bad config is caught in staging rather than in production" is stated as a settled outcome of an ordering property (drain finishes before flip). Ordering explains why no pool serves a half-applied config — a consistency guarantee. It does not explain why a bad config would be recognized as bad. The sentence spends the confidence of a safety claim on a mechanism that only established sequencing.
Flow break: Sentence 1 frames the problem as pools and quorums; sentence 2 pivots to "staging" and "production" without saying whether that's a separate deploy environment or just the pre-flip/post-flip state of the same pool. "The flip" also arrives with a definite article on its first mention, as if already introduced.
Concrete rewrite: Ask author: what actually validates the config before the flip — a schema check, a canary health probe, a required smoke test — that would let a bad value be caught rather than just delayed? And does "staging" name a separate environment, or the pool's own pre-flip drained state? Fallback without that fact: cut the second sentence, or if it must stay, drop "caught" for what the ordering actually supports — "a bad config stays inside the drained pool instead of reaching live traffic" — which is containment by timing, not detection.
Rewrite check: The ask-author question names the two missing facts (the validating mechanism, the meaning of "staging") without inventing either. The fallback line replaces "caught" with a containment claim the drain-before-flip ordering earns, and says so explicitly rather than quietly keeping the inflated verb.
