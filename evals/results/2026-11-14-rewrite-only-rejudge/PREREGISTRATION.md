# Pre-registration: rewrite-only re-judging of the subscription baseline

Revision 2, amended before any extraction, audit, or judgment. The original design
is preserved in commit `57f3c68d4e52079c762b7d7765e3df4d2f39d725`.
See [AMENDMENTS.md](AMENDMENTS.md) for the prospective repairs. `manifest.json` and
`prompt-templates.json` are generated by `scripts/rewrite_rejudge.py prepare` and
frozen with this file. No installed skill or baseline judgment is changed.

## Question

In the 2026-11-14 subscription baseline, task-fulfillment estimates favored the
skill in all eight apply-model × judge rows. Skill responses were also 2.6× (Sonnet)
and 1.8× (GPT) as long as no-skill responses. Most of the extra length is critique:
in the baseline outputs, skill responses averaged 359 words (Sonnet) and 158 (GPT),
while their `Concrete rewrite` fields averaged 82 and 49 words, against 137 and 87
words for whole no-skill responses. Judges saw the critique.

Does the skill's judged advantage survive when judges see only the deliverable the
writer would receive, without the critique?

## Inputs

- The frozen baseline archive `evals/results/2026-11-14-subscription-baseline/trials.tar.gz`
  (SHA-256 `ac9a292615299cd5160a08772118f1d76a2d5b273bb5b19e18b589be2c08dd23`). The
  manifest records the hashes of its `manifest.json` and `cases.json` and of every output
  used. The script refuses to run if any of them differ.
- No new apply outputs. Every response judged here was generated in the baseline.

## Scope

A case is in scope when its task asks for produced prose: the instruction (first line,
before any colon) contains rewrite, revise, tighten, or turn, the task does not require
the skill's Critique output format, and the task does not name the skill. Review-only
tasks are out of scope because their deliverable is the critique itself.

- **In scope:** 37 cases in 13 families. 16 have supplied facts and 21 have no
  additional facts. 7 are holdout cases, in 5 families.
- **Excluded:** 22 review-only or critique-format cases, listed in `manifest.json`.
- **Outputs:** 444 (37 cases × 2 apply models × 2 arms × 3 trials).

## Extraction

- One fixed extractor, `gpt-6.1-sol`, extracts every in-scope output in both arms with
  the prompt in `prompt-templates.json`. It copies the deliverable verbatim: replacement
  prose, questions for the author, or a cut/keep statement. It excludes diagnosis,
  rationale, self-checks, field labels and headings. Complete deliverable sentences/items
  must retain qualifications, uncertainty, attribution and hypothetical/conditional labels,
  including necessary context outside a rewrite field. Necessary qualifying context is
  not treated as separable rationale.
- **Validation:** every span must be an in-order, whitespace-normalized verbatim
  substring of the original response. A disposition other than `none` needs at least one
  span. An output that fails validation gets up to three attempts.
- **Rendering:** spans are joined by blank lines after the skill's template labels are
  removed from line starts (`Verdict:`, `Concrete rewrite:`, `Ask author:`, `Fallback N:`
  and the other template fields). The rule is the same for both arms.
- **Reliability:** 44 outputs (one in ten, chosen by opaque id) are also extracted by
  `claude-opus-5-5`. The exact-match rate, mean token Jaccard and disposition agreement
  are reported. They are not used to choose between extractions.

## Independent extraction audit (before judging)

Opus 5.5 audits every technically valid primary extraction in a fresh, tool-free context.
It sees the task, original response, disposition and rendered extraction, but no arm/model
identity, expected answer, scores, or alternative extraction. It checks completeness,
qualification preservation, absence of separable diagnosis/rationale, and correct
classification (including `none`). Every check requires boolean pass/fail and evidence.
This is a model audit of the measurement instrument, **not human fidelity calibration**.

All four checks must pass for every primary extraction before any judgment starts.
Missing/exhausted audits or a valid negative audit stop the study for review; a negative
check is an outcome, not a schema error, and cannot trigger another extraction or audit.
Do not silently exclude flagged outputs, select the other extractor, or relax this gate
once outputs exist. Publish the flagged artifacts and prepare review material. Any later
repair requires an explicit amendment and preserves the original successful responses.

## Views and judging

- **Views:** each in-scope output is judged in two views.
  - `full`: the baseline's blinded output, with `Rewrite check` and `Remembered line`
    already removed. Remaining format cues can still reveal an arm; opaque ids do not
    guarantee perceptual blinding.
  - `deliverable`: the rendered extraction, including an empty response when `none`.
    An empty response receives task fulfillment and rewrite quality 1; fidelity is judged
    against support/preservation rather than automatically called perfect or fabricated.
- **Judges:** both baseline judges (`claude-opus-5-5` and `gpt-6.1-sol`) score every
  view of every output. That is 1,776 judgments.
- **Prompt:** one judge prompt for both views, in `prompt-templates.json`. It carries the
  task, the supplied facts and the expected output. Legacy assertions and graded
  dimensions are not sent.
- **Dimensions:** the anchors are copied verbatim from the baseline.
  - Co-primary: `factual_fidelity` and `task_fulfillment`.
  - Secondary: `rewrite_quality`.
- **Blinding:** judges get new opaque ids that do not appear in the baseline, and job
  order is shuffled with seed 261118.

The full view under the new prompt is the control. The analysis exports the original
baseline comparison on the same retained cases, and the change in skill-minus-no-skill
advantage under the new prompt. Comparing the new full and deliverable views measures
view sensitivity: removal includes headings, surrounding explanation and extraction
choices, so it is not a pure causal estimate of critique or length.

## Exclusions

`none` is a substantive outcome and remains in both arms and both views; it is not
an exclusion. Only a technical extraction failure exhausted after three schema-invalid
attempts drops the case for that apply model, in both views, both arms, and all trials.
Report the failed-extraction count separately from **all case-wide excluded slots**
(six per affected case/model), by model and arm, including lost cases/families. If more
than 5% of the original 111 outputs in either model/arm cell are dropped, label that
model's comparison compromised. Any retained-case estimate is conditional on technical
completeness, not an unbiased estimate over missing cases. Never exclude a valid audit
failure or a weak writing outcome. Access/transport failures stop, rather than exclude.

## Analysis

For each apply model × judge × dimension:

1. **Case means:** trials are averaged within cases, separately for each arm.
2. **Family weighting:** cases are averaged within the 13 families, and each family
   carries equal weight.
3. **Estimates**, each using `subscription_baseline.stats` (paired bootstrap 95% CI and
   sign-flip test over family means):
   - `original_full`: original baseline skill minus no skill on the retained cases.
   - `Δ_full`: skill minus no skill, newly judged full view.
   - `Δ_deliverable`: skill minus no skill, deliverable view.
   - `view_attenuation`: `Δ_full − Δ_deliverable`, computed per case and family-weighted.
   - `prompt_shift`: `Δ_full − original_full`, also computed per case and family-weighted.
4. **Splits:** the primary analysis pools tune and holdout cases. Holdout-only estimates
   are reported, but with 5 families they are UNDERPOWERED by the gate's rule and are
   descriptive only.
5. **Fact conditions:** export all estimates separately for supplied and no-additional-facts
   cases as well as pooled. Preserve pair identity; pairs stay in their common family.
6. **Descriptive outputs:** lengths per view and arm, actual case-wide attrition, empty
   deliverables, independent audit receipts and extractor reliability. Intervals/tests are
   nominal, unadjusted descriptive comparisons, not a powered general efficacy gate.

## Interpretation rules (fixed now)

These labels apply per apply model, on `task_fulfillment`:

- **Positive under both judges:** the `Δ_deliverable` 95% CI lower bound is above 0
  under both judges.
- **Attenuated; deliverable benefit uncertain:** the `Δ_deliverable` 95% CI includes 0
  under both judges and `view_attenuation` has a 95% CI lower bound above 0 under both.
- **Mixed or uncertain:** anything else; report the judge-by-judge numbers.

Apply the same labels to `factual_fidelity`. Flag possible prose harm if either judge's
fidelity interval lies entirely below 0. The secondary dimension cannot upgrade labels.
A zero-crossing interval is not evidence of no benefit, no harm, or equivalence. No
"critique-driven" or equivalence claim is licensed by these rules. No labels apply if
the extraction audit is blocked or technical attrition exceeds the limit.

## What this study cannot establish

- **No doctrine promotion.** This is a measurement study on public, design-visible
  baseline cases. It cannot promote or reject doctrine and makes no general efficacy
  claim.
- **No judge calibration.** It does not resolve the Opus/GPT fidelity disagreement; the
  human calibration packet (`evals/results/2026-11-14-fidelity-calibration/`) remains
  the way to do that.
- **No causal length estimate.** Deliverable length still differs by arm and is
  reported, not adjusted.
- **No pure skill effect.** The extractor sees the whole response, so its choices could
  differ by arm. The verbatim rule limits it to copying, and the reliability subsample
  measures how much a second extractor would differ.

## Budget and stopping

- **Calls:** 488 extraction calls, up to 444 independent Opus audits, and (only after
  the audit gate passes) 1,776 judgments: at most 2,708 selected responses, plus up to
  three attempts per schema-invalid slot. No new apply outputs.
- **Estimated list-price equivalent:** about $80 including the new audits (baseline mean
  Opus judgment $0.047; GPT judgment $0.0089). Not a subscription invoice or API budget.
- **Authorization:** the owner requested continuation after the review/run proposal;
  the existing subscription-only, no separately billed API authorization applies. No
  provider substitution, credential copying, quota bypass, or new paid access.
- **Stopping:** any transport/access/model/rate-limit failure stops the run. Existing
  fatal attempts require explicit review, not automatic retries on resume. Only schema
  failures get replacement attempts. A complete retained response can be reparsed without
  regeneration. Valid negative extraction audits stop before judging as specified above.
